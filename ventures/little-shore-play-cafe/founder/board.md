# Board of Directors: Little Shore Play Cafe

**Vote: 3 FUND IF, 0 FUND, 0 PASS. Average score 4.7 / 10.**

Offers lens 5 (FUND IF) · Monopoly lens 4 (FUND IF) · Product lens 5 (FUND IF). Full memos in `founder/board/`.

Nobody passed. Nobody funded outright either. The board sees a real opening in Salisbury but no proof yet, and an idea that is trying to be too many things.

## Risks raised by more than one member

1. **Demand is unproven and the market is thin.** (Offers, Monopoly) About 6,261 under-5s in the county, median household income $72,861, free substitutes (library, zoo), and the founders' own model says weekday hours lose money. Nothing yet shows enough families will pay $59/month.
2. **Sponsorship is not an edge and should not be counted.** (All three) Offers: it is an offer to businesses, not parents, and none is confirmed. Monopoly: any copycat can sell the same signs. Product: logos on the play structures fight the calm Montessori brand.
3. **Too many products, no single core offer.** (Offers, Product) Membership, drop-in, three party tiers, rentals, classes, moms nights, loyalty, referrals and sponsorships are a price list, not an offer. The pitch reduces to "prettier than the free option, for $59."
4. **Retention after the founding rate.** (Monopoly, Offers) The only durable advantage is being first and being loved. Month 13, when $49 becomes $59, is the test.

## Where they disagreed

- **Price direction.** Offers asks whether membership should go *up* (around $79 with a weekly class and party credit stacked in). Product wants *fewer* mechanics and one simple price page. Pricing and offer skills test both.
- **Programming.** Monopoly sees the calendar and moms nights as the only network effect worth building. Product wants moms nights, craft clubs and character visits cut from Year 1.
- **Age range.** Product wants a narrower core band (under 5) and a floor plan that separates babies from older kids. The other two did not raise it.

## Conditions checklist (the rest of the pack tests these)

- [ ] **Demand before the lease:** 40 refundable founding deposits (the cap; above the ~32-family break-even) collected at pop-ups before signing. (Offers, Monopoly, Product) → `/founder-launch`, `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] **Survives Year 1 with zero sponsor money**; count a sponsor only once signed in writing. (Offers, Monopoly) → `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] **One named core offer** built around one outcome, with a real guarantee. (Offers) → `/founder-offer`
- [ ] **One simple price page** (drop-in, membership, party) and a written Year 1 cut list. (Product) → `/founder-pricing`, `/founder-offer`
- [ ] **Pre-sell parties** at posted prices before opening. (Offers) → `/founder-launch`
- [ ] **Lease protections:** use-exclusivity clause, renewal options, contingent on financing and permits. (Monopoly) → `/founder-ops`
- [ ] **Retention plan for month 13+** when the founding rate ends. (Monopoly) → `/founder-offer`, `/founder-marketing`
- [ ] **Sponsor design that does not put logos on play structures** (e.g., one sponsor wall), or no reliance on sponsors. (Product) → `/founder-brand`
- [ ] **Core age band + floor plan** separating babies, older kids, cafe seating and party room; a peak-hour noise and crowding plan. (Product) → `/founder-ops`
- [ ] **Written cleaning and opening checklist** with a named owner. (Product) → `/founder-ops`
- [ ] **Pop-up results reported against the core moment** (did parents sit, stay, return, pay a deposit). (Product) → `/founder-launch`
- [ ] Open numbers: NNN/CAM, insurance quote, occupancy limit, real competitor prices, county working-parent share. (All) → `/founder-competitors`, `/founder-ops`, `/founder-cfo`

## The strongest version the board can see

A membership-first neighborhood play cafe for under-5s, the only one in Salisbury, sold as **the calm, clean place a parent goes on a weekday morning or a rainy Saturday and actually sits down**. It launches only after 40 founding families have paid refundable deposits at pop-ups, runs one simple price page, leans on a few signature programs that build friendships among members (the only moat available), treats parties as the second engine, and treats sponsors as a bonus funded by one tasteful sponsor wall rather than logos on the play structures. That is narrower than the pitch: fewer programs, fewer discounts, a tighter age band, and no money counted that has not been signed.
