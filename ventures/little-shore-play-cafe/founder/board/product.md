# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The core moment is clear and good: a parent sits with a real coffee, in sight of a toddler absorbed in a calm, beautiful play space, and for once nobody is stressed. That moment is easy to picture, which is rare.
- The brand direction ("clean Montessori, upscale but not bougie, fun but not tacky") is a real point of view, and the team has a dedicated person for brand and design. That is where taste would come from.
- "Parents stay on site" is a smart simplification. It removes licensing and keeps the promise honest: this is a place to be with your child, not to leave them.
- Pop-up tests before a lease is the right order. It lets the experience be tried before it is built.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus. It is six businesses in one room.** Play space, cafe, party venue, class studio, moms-night craft workshop, sponsorship sales, plus memberships, drop-ins, founding rates, annual prepay, sibling pricing, a punch card, member discounts and referrals. A family-run team cannot do all of these brilliantly, and the brief does not say which one must be brilliant. The lens asks what to cut; the brief only adds.
2. **Sponsor logos fight the product.** The stated edge is a business name on each play structure. The stated brand is calm, clean Montessori. A "Perdue Farms" sign on the toddler kitchen is exactly the kind of visual noise that brand promises to remove. This edge trades the experience for build-out cash, and none of it is confirmed.
3. **The age range and room mix will break the calm.** Eight months to ten years in 2,000 to 2,500 sq ft, shared with a cafe and a party room that hosts up to 20 kids. Crawling babies, nine-year-olds and a Saturday party in one small space is the opposite of the Pinterest photo. Cleanliness and noise are the product here, and the plan has no visible answer for either at peak hours.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is Little Shore? If it needs "and" more than once, which half goes?
- Walk me through the first five minutes for a new parent with a 2-year-old on a Tuesday morning: parking, door, check-in, shoes, coffee, where they sit, what the child does first. Who greets them?
- What is the cleaning standard between sessions, who owns it, and how does a parent see that it happened?
- What happens to a member's quiet Saturday when a 20-kid Deluxe party is running? Do you close play, or share it?
- Would you open without any sponsor signage on the structures? If not, show me a rendering of a sponsored zone that still looks like your brand.
- What is the cafe, exactly, with no kitchen? Is the coffee good enough that a parent would come for it alone, and who makes it while also watching the floor?
- Where does a booking, membership or party hand off between people or systems (online signup, front desk, party host, cafe), and what does a parent see when that seam fails?
- Why 8 months to 10 years and not, say, under 5 only?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A one-sentence product statement, and a written cut list: what is not offered in Year 1 (likely moms nights, craft clubs, character visits, photo sessions, the Deluxe tier, and at least two of the five discount mechanics).
2. One simple price page: drop-in, membership, party. A parent should understand it in ten seconds.
3. A sponsorship design that does not put logos on play structures (for example, a single tasteful sponsor wall), shown as a rendering, or a plan that does not depend on sponsors at all.
4. A defined core age band and a floor plan that separates babies, older kids, cafe seating and the party room, with a stated plan for noise and peak-hour crowding.
5. Pop-up results reported against the core moment: did parents actually sit down, stay, come back and pay a deposit? Numbers, not likes.
6. A written cleaning and opening checklist with a named owner, before a lease is signed.
