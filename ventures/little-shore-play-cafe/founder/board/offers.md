# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The buyer is easy to find.** Parents of under-5s in one county gather in a few places: moms groups, churches, Instagram, library storytime. The cost to reach them should be low and word of mouth is natural.
- **Fast result, little effort.** The value equation favors the buyer on time and effort. The child is playing within minutes and the parent sits down with coffee. Nothing to learn and no long commitment.
- **Real scarcity exists.** "First 40 families at $49 for 12 months" is a true limit, not a fake one. It also matches the founders' own break-even of roughly 32 member families, so the founding offer doubles as the demand test.
- **Proof comes before the lease.** Pop-ups with refundable founding deposits are the right way to build the likelihood the offer lacks right now.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The market is "nice to have," not massive pain, and the money is thin.** Median household income of about $72,861 and a 14% poverty rate in a county of about 6,261 under-5s is a small, price-sensitive pool. Free options (library and zoo storytime) and an existing large play center already meet the core need. The brief says weekday hours lose money. A weak market is the most common reason this lens passes, and nothing yet shows enough families with this pain and this budget.
2. **There is no offer yet, only a price list.** Membership, drop-in, three party tiers, rentals, classes, moms nights, loyalty, referrals and sponsorships are listed but never stacked around one dream outcome. Likelihood is unproven (no pop-up data, no testimonials) and there is no guarantee. As written, the pitch to a parent is "cleaner and prettier than the free option, for $59 a month." That competes on aesthetics and drifts toward a commodity.
3. **The money is being chased from the wrong buyer.** The founders' stated edge, zone sponsorship, is an offer to businesses, not parents. None is confirmed, and the build-out partly assumes it. Year 1 loses money on the founders' own model, so if sponsors and parties lag, the membership offer has to carry everything, and it has not been tested.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What exact problem does the parent pay to solve: winter cabin fever, a place to work, adult connection, or school readiness? Pick one. The offer gets built around it.
- How many families put down a refundable deposit at the pop-ups, and at what price? Did the 40 founding slots fill?
- Why would a parent pay $59 a month when library storytime is free? What do they get here that they cannot get there?
- Can a parent actually "work" while staying fully responsible for a toddler? If not, drop that promise.
- What is the guarantee? For example: first month free if your child does not ask to come back, or money back if you use it fewer than a set number of times.
- Could you charge more (for example $79 with a weekly class and a party credit included) and still be the obvious choice? What would make it so?
- What share of revenue in the model depends on sponsorships and parties rather than memberships?
- Open unknowns that change the math: NNN/CAM costs, insurance quote, occupancy limit, real competitor prices, and the county's working-parent share.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Show demand before the lease.** The founding offer fills with refundable deposits at or above the 32-family break-even, from pop-ups and before signing.
2. **Build one named core offer.** Rebuild membership as a stack aimed at one outcome. For example: unlimited play, a weekly class, parent-night credits, a birthday-party discount and a priority booking window, all under one clear name. Each item should cost little to deliver and be worth a lot to the parent.
3. **Add a real guarantee** to the membership, so a parent takes on no risk by joining.
4. **Do not count on sponsors.** The plan has to survive opening and the first year with zero sponsorship revenue. Treat any sponsor money as upside.
5. **Pre-sell parties.** Book a meaningful number of parties ahead of opening at the posted prices to show the second revenue line is real.
