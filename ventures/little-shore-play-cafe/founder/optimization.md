# Year 1 optimization: Little Shore Play Cafe

Goal: the most Year 1 profit possible without losing what makes it work, a clean, modern, enchanting place where kids play and parents actually relax.

**Result: Year 1 operating result went from -$4,012 to +$30,290. Computed verdict went from Not yet to Profitable.** Same 100 buyer cards, same cost base checked by ops, every number from the founder-cfo tool.

## Guardrails (never traded for profit)

| Guardrail | Why it stays |
| --- | --- |
| Calm density: ~35 sq ft of play floor per child, about 39 kids at once in 2,500 sq ft | The "never packed" promise moved buyers from no to yes in every round. The old 25-kid cap was too tight, so it rose; it does not go to fire-code maximum. |
| Two staff on the floor at all times, plus Jordan | Cleanliness-first parents (90% buy in the final panel) said they want to *see* toys being wiped. |
| Visible clean standard (daily log, toy rotation, shoes off, sick-kid policy) | Collapsed the cleanliness objection from 13 of 76 non-buyers to near zero. |
| Coffee included, real seat with a sightline | It is the parent half of the promise; costs ~$0.60 a visit. |
| Gated Baby Nook | Answers the most-repeated fear ("big kids near my baby"). |
| Designed play village (catalog Montessori houses are fine; no cheap plastic) | The enchanting part. The lean build-out swaps custom builds for designed catalog houses, not for lower quality. |

## How the offer was tuned (all panels use seed 2026 so cards match)

| Round | Offer tested | Buyers | Buy | First-month visits | What it taught |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0 | As pitched: $59 membership, $12 + drink minimum | 100 | 24 | 39 | Membership above range; "+drink" read as included |
| 1 | v2: free first visit, $14 incl. coffee, 5-pack, $45 membership | 20 | 9 | 13 | Trust objections collapse |
| 2 | v3: per-child framing, monthly drink perk, $29 weekday tier | 20 | 7 | 9 | Perks unmentioned; sibling cost became salient |
| 3A | Full offer at $15 flat, plain copy | 20 | 8 | 14 | $15 sits on the price ceiling |
| 3B | Weekday $13 / weekend $16 | 20 | 11 | 18 | Weekday price lifts buyers |
| 3C | $15 flat, experience-led copy ("They play. You sit. Right there.") | 20 | 10 | 17 | Copy alone adds buyers and doubles party interest |
| 4D | Experience copy + weekday $13 / weekend $16 | 20 | 11 | 19 | Best demand; $16 weekend draws objections |
| **Final** | **Experience copy + weekday $13 / weekend $15, $60 any-day pack, parties $325/$395** | **100** | **71** | **137** | 47 new buyers vs round 0, 0 lost |

Read the buy rates as upper bounds: simulated buyers lean agreeable, and many counted the free first visit as a purchase. The stricter signal is **paid visits per 100 parents in month one: about 39 as pitched vs about 66 now (+70%).** The model uses a smaller lift than that.

Party panel (10 birthday planners): acceptable Basic party price $151 to $400, sweet spot ~$250. 8 of 10 said "it isn't open before my child's birthday", so **party pre-sales only start once a lease and opening date are firm**, with a refundable deposit.

## What is still not solved

- **Debt plus owner pay.** With a $150k loan (~$1,941/mo) and $3,000/mo for Jordan, break-even rises to **40 paying family visits a day** and Year 1 is -$29,002 (steady state +$2,095/mo). At 55 a day it is +$10,154 in Year 1. The pop-ups still have to prove demand above 45 a day before a lease.
- **Free-activity loyalists bought at 0% in every round.** 15% of local parents are not customers at any tested price. Do not market to them.
- **All costs are estimates** except the rent quote. Replace with real quotes before lenders see this.
