# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market that one player can own.** The brief found no play cafe in Salisbury, and the nearest similar businesses are 30 to 85 miles away. A town of about 33,000, in a county with about 6,261 children under 5, probably supports one play cafe and not two. Whoever opens first and fills the membership base takes the slot, and that is the right shape for this lens: start with a big share of a small market.
- **Distribution is planned before the product.** Pop-ups with refundable founding deposits, moms groups, churches and sponsor cross-promotion give a real path to the first 100 families before a lease is signed. Most local concepts skip this step.
- **Memberships can build a weak network effect.** If families come on the same weekdays, parents form friendships there. That pulls in other parents and makes them less likely to leave. This is the only network effect available, and the programming calendar and moms nights point the right way.
- **Sensible sizing.** Choosing 2,000 to 2,500 sq ft over 4,500 because profit by Year 3 is similar shows the founders are not trying to win on scale they cannot afford.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing stops a copycat, and the slot only holds one.** There is no proprietary know-how, no technology and no scale advantage. The "Montessori-inspired" look can be copied with a Pinterest board and a build-out budget. If a better-funded operator, or CoCo's Funhouse adding a toddler zone, opens nearby in year one or two, the two of them split a market that only supports one. The model's break-even of about 32 member families shows how thin the margin is. The only defense is getting full first and being loved, so speed and member retention are what protect the business.
2. **The stated edges are not edges.** Sponsorship of play zones is a funding method, not a moat, and any competitor can sell the same signs. None of it is confirmed. A full programming calendar is also easy to copy. Neither one is ten times better than the next option on something parents care about. Today the honest answer to "what is the secret?" is "no one else has done it here yet." That buys a head start, not durability.
3. **The real competitor may be free or already paid for.** Library storytime and zoo story time are free. Daycare and preschool take up weekday hours for working parents, and the brief says the working-parent share is unknown. If weekday mornings stay empty (and the founders' own model says slow weekday hours lose money), the business depends on weekends and parties. There it does compete head on with CoCo's Funhouse.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What can you do in the first 12 months that a copycat could not do in month six? Exclusive lease terms, a long lease in the best location, a locked founding cohort?
- How many refundable deposits do the pop-ups need to collect before you sign, and what does each family cost to acquire?
- What do you know about Salisbury parents that an outside chain operator would not? Write it in one sentence.
- How many families stay after the founding rate ends at month 13? What is the plan if half of them leave?
- If CoCo's Funhouse adds a toddler area, which part of your offer still wins?
- What share of under-5 children in the county are in full-time care on weekdays? (Open question; not in the brief.)
- What is the real all-in rent once NNN and CAM are known, and does break-even stay near 32 families?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pop-ups collect refundable founding deposits from at least 40 families (the founding-rate cap) before a lease is signed. This proves distribution and the first part of the monopoly.
2. The lease includes a use-exclusivity clause for the center, plus renewal options long enough to protect the location.
3. At least one sponsorship is signed in writing before it is counted toward build-out funding.
4. A written retention plan for after month 12 (community events, a member-only calendar, a priced plan for the end of the founding rate), because member stickiness is the only durability this business can build.
